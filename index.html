2 · Print options

Plates open in Bambu Studio with Lego settings already set (256 mm beds: P1S, P1P, X1, P2S): 3 walls, Arachne walls printed outside-first, seam at the back, 15% infill, no brim, supports off, travel avoids crossing walls. 0.20 mm clutches slightly better; 0.10 mm looks smoother.

Gap is the space between parts: smaller gaps mean shorter travel moves and less stringing. Keep scale at 100% for bricks that join real ones; if they fit too tight or loose, use the fit test below, which adjusts the slicer's X-Y compensation instead (scaling would also shift the 8 mm stud spacing).

Before printing: wash the textured plate with hot water and dish soap (finger grease is the usual first-layer failure, and the first layer decides the fit). Use plain PLA (Basic or PLA+); matte and silk PLA are more brittle. On a P1S with PLA, open the door or lift the top glass so the hotend doesn't clog from heat. Very small parts (minifigure hands, tiny clips) print poorly with a 0.4 mm nozzle. Print at Standard speed: Sport and Ludicrous lose detail on small parts.

Automatic supports: every underside is checked by geometry. Bridges up to 40 mm and overhangs up to about 70° print as they are; a part that fails is turned onto its best side, then gets breakaway pillars under what still overhangs, and fins from Support Fins. Print with slicer supports off.

Fit test

Before a big set, print a 2×4 brick and a 2×4 plate (about 20 minutes), try them on real bricks, and tell Set2Print how they fit. It adjusts the settings for every build; repeat until both are just right. Printed bricks grip each other harder than they grip real ones (their layer lines interlock), so also try two printed parts together. Fit changes with filament brand and even colour, so test again when you switch. If only the bottom edge of the underside is tight, that is first-layer flare: raise elephant-foot compensation in Bambu Studio (Quality, Precision) from 0.15 mm toward 0.2 mm.
